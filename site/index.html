Lessons that travel without internet

Titip turns a lesson into one small file. It passes from phone to phone, opens with no connection, lets a learner practise with hints, and sends a short code back so the teacher knows what to teach again.

A learner wants to practise at home. The practice is behind a link, and the family phone is not online.

How it works

Write once. Pass it on. Hear back.

1

Write

The teacher fills in one form: the lesson text, multiple-choice questions, a one-sentence hint for each, and optional word meanings. Or copies one of 11 ready-made lessons.

2

Pass it on

One file (~148 KB raw, ~52 KB gzipped) holds the whole app and the lesson. It goes by Bluetooth, WhatsApp or memory card and opens in any phone browser with no internet. No phone at home? Print the worksheet.

3

Hear back

The learner gets a short code such as HI-1132-0 and sends it by SMS or on paper. It holds no name. The teacher types the codes in and sees first-try accuracy, the top wrong answer, and per-choice error counts.

For the learner

  • Read the lesson, or press Listen to hear it in its own language
  • Tap an underlined word (Latin, Devanagari, Bengali or Arabic script) to see what it means
  • A wrong answer gives a hint and another try; missed questions come back once at the end
  • Larger text and a dark high-contrast theme, remembered on the device
  • Works by keyboard alone; feedback is never shown by colour only
  • Full interface in 9 languages: English, Indonesian, Spanish, French, Portuguese, Swahili, Hindi, Bengali and Arabic (RTL)

For the teacher

  • 11 sample lessons across all 9 languages, editable and re-exportable
  • Results per question: right on first try, most common wrong answer, and per-choice error chips
  • One tap builds a follow-up lesson from the questions to teach again
  • Compare a second round after teaching again, side by side
  • Save rounds on the device, export a spreadsheet, print a class record sheet
  • Printed worksheet with the answer key upside down at the foot

Evidence

Every design choice traces to a number someone else measured

29 research papers and 59 global and national sources across Africa, South Asia, Latin America and Southeast Asia were read for this project. These three shaped the design most.

g = 0.499

Average gain from practice quizzes across 222 classroom studies. So every lesson ends in questions, and missed ones come back.

Yang et al., Psychological Bulletin, 2021

0.49 vs 0.05

Effect of feedback that explains, against feedback that only says right or wrong. So each wrong answer shows a short hint.

Van der Kleij et al., Review of Educational Research, 2015

0.30–0.35 SD

Learning gain across 16,936 pupils in India, Kenya, Nepal, Philippines and Uganda via targeted phone/SMS tutoring; SMS alone had no effect in Botswana. So the code goes back to a teacher.

Angrist et al., Nature, 2026

Where similar tools failed, and what Titip does about it

  • An offline platform on tablets in 102 schools in Honduras did not raise scores; the evaluators point to late hardware and too little teacher training (IPA). Titip needs no hardware delivery and no platform training.
  • Access to technology alone does not raise learning (World Bank Research Observer, 2022). Titip is built around practice and a teacher's follow-up, not around access.
  • Elaborate feedback can do worse than simple feedback (Frontiers in Education, 2025). Hints are one sentence.

What these numbers are not

They come from other tools in other places. They are reasons to expect Titip to help, not proof that it does. No test with real learners has been run yet.
